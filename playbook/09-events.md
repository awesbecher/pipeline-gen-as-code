## How the best run it

The event starts four to six weeks before the event. Build the attendee
list yourself from speakers, exhibitors, and LinkedIn signals, then score
it against ICP: RSAC 2026 published 43,500 plus attendees, and Andrew's
filter cuts that to under 2,000 real targets, a heuristic that moves
entirely with your ICP. Sequence the top tier with angles specific to
their session or stack. The specific note beats the generic "we will be
at RSA" note; the closest measured support is email personalization,
where advanced personalization replies at 17 to 18 percent against 7 to 9
for basic. The 2 versus 10 percent split Andrew quotes for event notes is
his number; no source publishes it. Pre-booking at least half the
meetings before the floor opens is his operating rule too; Vendelux
publishes pre-booked meetings only in absolute terms, 80 to 200 per major
show. The highest-trust conversations happen off the floor: a 12 to 20
seat executive-hosted dinner prices at $1,000 to $3,000 per qualified
meeting against $1,500 to $3,000 for a Tier 1 sponsorship, so at worst it
ties and at best it wins on cost. That the host's title decides who
accepts is Andrew's read, not a measured finding. Then the SLA that names
the engine: hot leads touched inside 24 hours, everything inside 48. Those
two lines are Andrew's; the decay underneath them is measured, since the
odds of qualifying a lead drop sharply within the first hour.

## The number

Published cost per qualified opportunity runs $5,000 to $15,000 for
disciplined programs and $10,000 to $25,000 for average ones, cut by
program discipline rather than by booth versus pre-booking. Andrew runs
to $2,500 to $5,000 per opportunity, which sits below every published
band and is an operator heuristic. Event-touched deals do close a little
better: 12.1 percent creation to closed-won for live events and 13.8 for
virtual against 11.1 percent for other channels, on far fewer created
deals.

Sources: claims EV-1 to EV-7 in docs/SOURCES.md (evidence class and
confidence per claim; unsourced values are Andrew operator heuristics).

## Run it when

Enterprise ACV ($75K plus) and monthly pipeline budget at $15K or more.
Under that, attend rather than sponsor; a dinner beats a booth anyway.
A no-events-budget constraint blocks it outright.

## First 90 days

Weeks 1 to 4: pick one or two ICP-dense events for the quarter. Start
building the attendee list from speakers, exhibitors, and signals.
Weeks 5 to 8: score the list against ICP, sequence the top tier with
session-specific or stack-specific angles, and book the dinner with the
most senior host you can field.
Weeks 9 to 12: run the floor against the pre-booked calendar, capture
everything, and hold the 24/48 SLA while the inbox is still warm.

## Tripwires

Fewer than half the meetings pre-booked at doors-open: the program is a
booth, not an engine; next quarter goes ICP-first or nowhere. Follow-up
sliding past the 48-hour line: the spend already evaporated; fix the SLA
before booking another event.
