## How the best run it

The event starts four to six weeks before the event. Build the attendee
list yourself from speakers, exhibitors, and LinkedIn signals, score it
against ICP (RSA's 43,000 badges reduce to under 2,000 real targets),
and sequence the top tier with angles specific to their session or
stack; the generic "we will be at RSA" note replies at 2 percent, the
specific one near 10. The best programs pre-book half their meetings
before the floor opens, and the highest-trust conversations happen off
the floor: a 12 to 20 seat executive-hosted dinner outperforms the
booth, and the host's title decides who accepts. Then the SLA that names
the engine: hot leads touched inside 24 hours, everything inside 48,
because the inbox moves on.

## The number

Booth-first programs run 8 to 12 thousand dollars per opportunity;
ICP-first with pre-booking runs 2.5 to 5.

## Stack

Luma (hosted events) · Instantly (pre-event sequencing) · Clay (list
building) · Ergo (capture and sync)

## Run it when

Enterprise ACV ($75K plus) and monthly pipeline budget at $15K or more.
Under that, attend rather than sponsor; a dinner beats a booth anyway.
A no-events-budget constraint blocks it outright.

## First 90 days

Weeks 1 to 4: pick one or two ICP-dense events for the quarter. Start
building the attendee list from speakers, exhibitors, and signals.
Weeks 5 to 8: score the list against ICP, sequence the top tier with
session-specific or stack-specific angles, and book the dinner with the
most senior host you can field.
Weeks 9 to 12: run the floor against the pre-booked calendar, capture
everything, and hold the 24/48 SLA while the inbox is still warm.

## Tripwires

Fewer than half the meetings pre-booked at doors-open: the program is a
booth, not an engine; next quarter goes ICP-first or nowhere. Follow-up
sliding past the 48-hour line: the spend already evaporated; fix the SLA
before booking another event.
